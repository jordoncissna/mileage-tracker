Questions people actually ask

Milo Mileage Tracker · last updated September 10, 2026
The one-line version: Milo is a place to write down business drives so that, if you're ever asked to prove them, you can. You type the trips; it does the arithmetic, the receipts and the report.

How it works

Does Milo track my drives automatically in the background?

No. Milo has never done this, and we'd rather say so plainly than let you find out after a month of missing trips. Every trip in your log is there because you entered it. Milo helps by looking up addresses, calculating the distance, remembering routes you drive often, and reminding you about days with nothing logged — but the drive itself is something you record.

Automatic tracking needs a real phone app with background location permission. That isn't built yet.

How long does logging a trip actually take?

A few seconds once your common places are saved. Type part of an address and pick from the suggestions — addresses you've driven before come up first. Milo fills in the distance. If you drove there and back, one toggle makes it a round trip: one line item with the miles doubled, not two rows.

What if I forget to log a day?

Home shows recent days with nothing against them, and one tap starts a trip dated to that day. It only ever says a day is empty — it never claims you drove. It learns which weekdays you actually log, so it won't nag about a Sunday you never work, and there's a switch in Settings to turn it off.

This matters more than it sounds. The IRS asks for records kept close to the time. A log written the same week holds up; one reconstructed at tax time is the part that gets challenged.

Can I bring in trips from a spreadsheet?

Yes — History → Import, from a CSV with at least a Date and a Miles column. Rows that are already in your log are skipped rather than duplicated, and rows with an unreadable date or an impossible mileage are rejected instead of quietly landing in your tax records.

The tax side

What does the IRS actually want?

For vehicle expenses, IRC §274(d) wants each trip to show the date, where you went, the business purpose, and the mileage — kept at or near the time of the drive. Milo's report prints exactly those four things for every trip, plus the rate and the deduction.

Is a mileage log enough on its own?

For the driving, generally yes. For the toll, the parking, or the client lunch, it isn't — those are separate expenses and an auditor asks for the receipt. That's why you can attach a photo to any trip, and why the tax report prints those photos after the log as numbered exhibits. One file, mileage plus evidence.

Can I change the mileage rate?

Yes, per year, in Settings. Milo ships with the IRS standard rates and you can override any of them.

Can I use Milo outside the United States? You can use it anywhere, and the trips you record are your own records wherever you live. But Milo is built for US federal rules — the rates are IRS rates, the amounts are US dollars, and the report cites US tax code. If you file elsewhere, replace the rates with your own and treat the report as a mileage log rather than a finished tax document. Per-country rules don't exist yet.

Is Milo tax advice?

No. It keeps records; it doesn't tell you what you may deduct. What counts as a deductible business trip is between you and a tax professional. See the Terms.

Your data

Who can see my trips?

You. Your records are stored with per-account access rules, so one account can never read another's rows, and receipt photos live in a private bucket reachable only through a link that expires in five minutes. Traffic is encrypted in transit and at rest.

To be precise about what that does not mean: it isn't end-to-end encrypted. The server holds your data in a form it could read. We don't sell it, we don't run ads, and we don't track you around the web — the Privacy Policy spells this out.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Nothing. Your trips live in your account, not on the device. Sign in anywhere and they're there.

How do I get my data out?

History → Export gives you a CSV of exactly what the ledger is showing, filters included. The tax report gives you a printable PDF with the receipts embedded. Both are free forever, on every plan — they're your own tax substantiation, and we won't put them behind a payment. That rule is written into the Terms and enforced by the tests.

How do I delete my account?

Settings → Delete my account. It removes every trip, every receipt photo and every setting from our servers, then signs you out. No email, no waiting on anyone. There is no restore — export your CSV or tax report first if you want a copy.

Does Milo work offline?

Yes. It installs to your home screen and keeps working with no signal; trips sync when you're back online.
